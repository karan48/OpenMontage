# Script Guide: Kids Story (Hinglish cartoon stories — long-form episodes and Shorts)

Read this after `skills/creative/storytelling.md`. Where they conflict on register, structure or humor level, this guide wins.

*Draft. Lock it after the first two episodes.*

## Voice and register

A playful storyteller who knows the punchline is coming and can barely hold it in. Lines are short, everyday Hinglish, and say-able in one breath.

- "Pappu ने सोचा — exam में सिर्फ़ एक दिन बचा है… perfect time to start padhai."
- "और फिर… mummy आ गईं."

Characters speak in their own voice, which is defined in `characters.md`. Keep dialogue to 1-2 lines per turn.

## Write action beats, not narration

This is the character-animation pipeline. **Every beat must be something a character does**: a pose, an action, a reaction. If a line can't be acted, cut it or turn it into an action. Annotate each beat with the character, the action and the emotion, so the rig and pose library can perform it.

## Long-form structure (4-8 min episodes, draft)

The same cast and the same rules, with room for a real story arc.

| Part | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0-20 s | **Cold-open gag:** a quick, self-contained joke that sets the episode's theme. |
| Setup | What the lead wants this episode, and why it matters to them. |
| Acts 1-3 | Each act is a **bigger problem**. The lead's plan fails in a funnier way each time, and supporting characters complicate it. |
| Twist | The unexpected turn, usually set up in act 1. |
| Payoff + moral | Shown through what happens, never preached. A callback to the cold open lands well here. |
| End tag | A last 5-10 s gag after the moral. |

Keep at most three characters in a scene, even in long episodes. Write and sample act 1 before committing the full episode.

## Shorts structure (40-60 s)

A Short is a standalone mini-story, or one self-contained gag cut down from an episode.

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0-2 s | **Hook:** the lead is already in a funny or strange situation. |
| 2-12 s | **Setup:** what they want. |
| 12-35 s | **Conflict:** it goes wrong, then goes wrong again. |
| 35-50 s | **Twist:** the unexpected turn. |
| 50-60 s | **Payoff** plus a one-line moral, shown not preached, ideally a callback to the hook. |

## Humor

High. Situational comedy from relatable Indian life (exams, mummy, neighbours, office, cricket, relatives), slapstick, comic timing, and held reaction poses. Leave a beat of silence before the punchline.

## Never

- Crude or adult humor. The audience includes kids.
- Mocking real people, communities or religions.
- A character drifting from its design sheet.
- More than three characters in one Short.
